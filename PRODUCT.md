# 5 Past 12 — Product Definition

## Mission

**5 Past 12 exists to make the consequences of climate change visible, understandable, and difficult to ignore.**

The project should help people understand what is already happening, what may affect their own region, how strong the scientific evidence is, and what meaningful actions are available.

The intended emotional response is not manufactured panic. The goal is evidence-based urgency: if the underlying facts are alarming, the product should not dilute them. The emotional weight should come from verified reality, clear visualisation, and proximity to the user.

## Tagline

> **The crisis isn't coming. It's happening now.**

## Problem

Climate change is one of the largest long-term threats to human societies. Its effects are already visible and are expected to intensify as warming continues.

At the same time, many people experience the issue as distant, abstract, fragmented, or easy to overlook. Extreme events appear as isolated news stories. Scientific publications are difficult to access and interpret. Long-term trends are separated from immediate events. Climate-related reporting competes with many other headlines and often lacks continuity.

As a result, users may lack a coherent view of:

- what is currently happening,
- how climate change is connected to a specific event,
- how strong that connection is scientifically,
- which effects are relevant to their own region,
- what meaningful responses are available.

## Solution

5 Past 12 is a public climate information platform centred around a live visual representation of current and recent climate-related events.

Users should be able to:

1. see climate-related events globally,
2. focus on events and climate signals in a selected region,
3. understand what happened and why it matters,
4. inspect the evidence supporting climate-related claims,
5. access relevant scientific publications and source material,
6. understand how risks may affect them locally,
7. find practical ways to prepare and meaningful ways to act.

AI is used to make scientific information understandable and relevant. It is not used to invent facts, replace scientific evidence, or manufacture certainty.

## Product Principles

### 1. Evidence before narrative

Every scientific climate claim must be supported by retrieved evidence.

If a claim cannot be supported, it must not be generated.

If an event cannot be linked to climate change with sufficient evidence, it must not be presented as a climate-impact event.

### 2. Scientific sources outrank news coverage

Scientific publications, attribution studies, official datasets, and other high-quality evidence form the basis of climate claims.

News may provide context, reported impacts, imagery, and timelines, but it must not substitute for scientific attribution evidence.

### 3. AI is an interpretation layer

AI may:

- summarise scientific material,
- extract relevant evidence,
- translate technical findings into accessible language,
- identify relevant passages,
- structure information,
- generate region-specific explanations from verified data.

AI may not independently establish facts that are absent from the evidence supplied to it.

### 4. Urgency without sensationalism

The product should not soften serious findings merely to appear neutral or reassuring.

At the same time, it must not exaggerate uncertainty, causality, impacts, or scientific confidence for emotional effect.

### 5. Local relevance

The climate crisis should not be presented only as something happening elsewhere.

Users should be able to enter a region and understand nearby events, climate signals, and relevant risks without sharing precise location data.

### 6. Action beyond individual consumption

5 Past 12 should not reduce climate action to personal carbon-footprint optimisation.

Where relevant, it should help users understand:

- how to protect themselves from climate hazards,
- how communities can prepare,
- how to participate in public processes,
- how to support credible organisations,
- and how political and economic decision-making can be influenced through legitimate civic channels.

### 7. Public information, not engagement maximisation

5 Past 12 is not intended to maximise retention, screen time, notifications, or behavioural engagement.

The product should help users become informed and then leave with a clearer understanding of reality and available actions.

## Non-Commercial Commitment

5 Past 12 is a public-interest and social-good project.

The project is not intended to generate revenue now or in the future.

Continued development should preserve the following commitments:

- remain open source,
- no paid access,
- no subscriptions,
- no advertising business model,
- no sale of user data,
- no monetisation of user attention,
- no premium information tier,
- no commercialisation of access to climate evidence.

Technical or organisational decisions should not undermine this mission.

## Feature Overview

### Core

#### Live global climate view

The landing page should immediately show an interactive globe or global map containing current or recently occurred climate-related events.

The experience should make the global scale of climate impacts visible without requiring the user to navigate through introductory pages first.

#### Regional relevance

Users can enter a region or location manually.

The application should then prioritise nearby or regionally relevant events and information.

No automatic geolocation or persistent storage of location is required.

#### Event detail view

Each event should have a dedicated view containing, where available:

- what happened,
- where and when it happened,
- observed impacts,
- immediate causes or contributing factors,
- the scientifically supported relationship to climate change,
- relevant evidence,
- linked scientific sources.

#### Claim-to-evidence traceability

Scientific claims should be connected to their supporting evidence.

Where technically possible, the interface should link not only to the source publication but to the relevant section, passage, page, or extracted evidence block.

#### Evidence-grounded AI explanations

AI-generated explanations must be produced from structured, retrieved evidence and must not introduce unsupported claims.

### Target Scope

#### Regional climate signals

The regional view may include climate indicators or anomalies even when no major disaster is occurring nearby.

#### Why this matters to you

The system should explain why a global or regional event is relevant to the region selected by the user, but only when this can be supported by available data.

#### Preparedness guidance

For supported hazard types, the application may provide practical preparedness information based on credible sources.

#### Action pathways

The application may present credible ways to participate in civic, community, political, or economic action without prescribing party-political choices.

#### Evidence confidence

Where supported by the available research, the UI may communicate evidence quality, attribution confidence, or uncertainty.

## Primary User Journey

1. User opens 5 Past 12.
2. The user immediately sees current and recent climate-related events on the globe.
3. The user explores a major event.
4. The event page explains what happened and shows the scientific basis for its climate relevance.
5. The user can inspect the underlying evidence and source publications.
6. The user enters their own region.
7. The application surfaces locally relevant events or climate signals.
8. The user sees practical protection measures and, where available, meaningful ways to act.

## MVP Success Criteria

The MVP is successful if a first-time user can answer the following after a short session:

- What important climate-related events are happening now?
- What is happening near the region I care about?
- What evidence links a specific event to climate change?
- Where does that evidence come from?
- What can I do to prepare or act?

The experience should feel scientifically grounded, visually compelling, and immediately understandable.
